# Historical reasoning task A

**Knowledge cutoff:** 31 December 1904. Use only the resources stated below. This is a modernized problem sheet about resources available by that date; the formulas are given in modern notation and are not claimed to be verbatim excerpts from one historical paper. Do not consult other texts.

In mechanics, uniform translation cannot be detected by ordinary internal mechanical experiments. The conventional Galilean coordinate change uses (x'=x-vt) and (t'=t). Electromagnetic field theory contains a characteristic vacuum propagation speed (c); the Galilean change does not preserve the same light propagation relation in both moving coordinate systems.

Lorentz's work supplies a different family of coordinate relations. One normalized mathematical form is

\[
x'=\gamma(x-vt),\qquad t'=\gamma(t-vx/c^2),\qquad
\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}.
\]

At speeds small relative to (c), this is close to the familiar mechanical approximation. In an ether-based interpretation, one coordinate system retains a privileged rest status; moving rods and clocks can undergo physical effects that help account for the observed relations. Experiments seeking simple ether drift have not delivered a stable direct measurement of such motion. They do not, by themselves, eliminate every preferred-frame theory.

Distant clocks can be compared by exchanging light signals, provided a rule for assigning the outward and return travel times is stated. A clock reading at one location and the assignment of a time to a distant event are different operations.

**Task:** Propose a physically coherent interpretation of the given coordinate relations. Identify the inherited assumption you would inspect, the operation that changes the model, the empirical or mathematical successes to retain, at least one competing interpretation still possible, and one next calculation or observation that would discriminate claims if possible. State explicitly when the available facts leave two interpretations empirically unresolved. Do not invoke evidence or concepts first developed after the cutoff as construction inputs.
