# Historical reasoning task B

**Knowledge cutoff:** immediately before the 1859–1860 work on molecular motion in gases. Use only the resources stated below. This is a modernized problem sheet, not a transcription of one historical document. Do not consult other texts.

Classical mechanics describes collisions through positions, velocities, forces, and conservation of energy and momentum. A kinetic picture relates gas pressure to many molecular impacts on the container walls. Macroscopic pressure and temperature remain stable and reproducible even though the sample contains an enormous number of molecules whose individual paths are inaccessible.

The ideal-gas relation and equilibrium measurements constrain any microscopic account. Probability mathematics and symmetry reasoning are available as mathematical tools. The gas has no observed preferred spatial direction at equilibrium. Those facts alone do not specify the exact motion of any individual molecule, and molecular independence cannot simply be assumed without an argument or a stated approximation.

**Task:** Propose a tractable description that connects the mechanical picture to measurable gas properties. Identify the inherited explanatory assumption you would inspect, one local change of representation, the mechanical and thermodynamic successes to retain, at least one competing route or unresolved assumption, and a calculation or measurement that would test the proposal. Keep any proposed probability law conditional on its stated assumptions. Do not use developments after the cutoff as if they were already established.
