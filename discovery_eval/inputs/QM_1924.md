# Historical reasoning task C

**Knowledge cutoff:** 31 December 1924. Use only the resources stated below. This is a modernized problem sheet, not a transcription of one historical document. Do not consult other texts.

The old atomic theory combines classical electron orbits with additional action-quantization rules. It accounts for hydrogen and selected simple spectra, yet general rules for more complex atoms and observed line intensities remain unsettled. Experiments report discrete emitted or absorbed frequencies and relative intensities. The Ritz combination relation connects frequencies of transitions between stationary energy labels: (\nu_{mn}=(E_m-E_n)/h).

Classical periodic motion can be written as Fourier components, and Hamiltonian mechanics relates motion to energy. The correspondence principle requires appropriate recovery of successful classical relations. De Broglie's 1924 proposal also associates a wavelength (\lambda=h/p) with matter. These are available clues, not an established dynamics for microscopic states.

**Task:** Propose one or more mathematically tractable ways to represent microscopic states and observable changes while preserving the successful spectral and classical limits. Identify which assumption of orbit-based reasoning you would question, the local operation that generates each candidate, a serious alternative route, unresolved costs, and the next calculation that could distinguish or connect the candidates. Do not use later formalism or experiments as reasons available at the cutoff.
