# Historical reasoning task E

**Knowledge cutoff:** 31 December 1911, before Einstein and Grossmann's 1912–13 mathematical work on gravitation. Use only the resources stated below. This is a modernized problem sheet, not a verbatim historical document. Do not consult other texts.

Newtonian gravity accurately describes many ordinary orbits and free-fall motions. Special relativity treats uniform inertial motion without a universal time shared by all observers. Equality of inertial and gravitational mass suggests that, in a sufficiently small freely falling laboratory, ordinary gravitational acceleration can disappear from local experiments. An accelerated laboratory without gravity can produce locally similar effects. Gravitational time or light-propagation effects have been proposed, but their detailed laws remain unsettled.

The local comparison has a limit: two nearby falling bodies in a nonuniform gravitational field need not maintain the same separation. Changing the motion of one laboratory cannot remove all such relative effects over an extended region. Mathematical tools exist for describing distances that vary from place to place, but no unique gravitational field equation follows merely from possessing those tools. Relativistic scalar or force-based accounts remain possible competitors.

There is also a rotating-frame question: a description that treats centrifugal effects as gravity must explain measurements of rods and clocks in that frame while retaining the local successes of special relativity. This is an available line of inquiry, not a supplied geometric solution.

**Task:** Suggest a physically meaningful next conceptual representation for gravity that connects local free fall with the surviving relative effects. Explain why local equivalence alone does not force your choice, which Newtonian and relativistic successes it should retain, and what would distinguish it from a nongeometric competitor. Do not use the 1913 Entwurf theory, the 1915 field equations or Mercury calculation, or later eclipse and wave evidence as construction inputs.
