# Historical reasoning task F

**Knowledge cutoff:** 31 December 1968. Use only the resources stated below. This is a modernized problem sheet, not a verbatim historical document. Do not consult other texts.

Near some continuous phase transitions, correlations extend over many length scales and simple spatially uniform mean-field calculations give the wrong critical behavior. Quite different materials can nevertheless display similar power laws. Phenomenological scaling hypotheses relate several measured exponents but do not, by themselves, calculate why a given set of exponents appears. Kadanoff's 1966 construction groups many Ising spins into cells smaller than the correlation length and uses each cell's collective magnetization; repeated grouping suggests a change of description with scale. In quantum field theory, some coupling parameters are known to depend on the scale at which observations are described.

A microscopic model contains both short- and long-wavelength fluctuations. Dropping the short-wavelength variables outright risks losing their influence on long-distance measurements. Retaining every microscopic variable makes the critical calculation intractable. The form of a coarse-grained description may also acquire interactions absent from a simple initial approximation.

**Task:** Propose one explicit, repeatable transformation between descriptions at different scales that addresses this dilemma. Explain how it preserves long-distance predictions, how a competing scaling-only approach differs, and what further result would distinguish a useful explanatory transformation from a formal relabeling. Do not assume the 1971 Wilson papers, their fixed-point classification, or later exponent calculations as given results.
