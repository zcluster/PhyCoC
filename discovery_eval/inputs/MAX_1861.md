# Historical reasoning task D

**Knowledge cutoff:** 31 December 1861, before the January 1862 continuation of Maxwell's *On Physical Lines of Force*. Use only the resources stated below. This is a modernized problem sheet, not a verbatim historical document. Do not consult other texts.

Steady electric currents produce magnetic effects. Faraday's induction experiments show that changing magnetic conditions can produce electrical effects. A model of magnetic lines as local motions or stresses in a medium is available, but its microscopic mechanism is not established. Electrostatic charging can persist across a material that conducts very little: a Leyden jar, for example, can be charged even though its glass does not carry an ordinary sustained conduction current. Materials differ greatly in their ability to conduct and to respond electrically to an applied force.

An account using only charges moving through conductors has difficulty giving the changing electrical condition of an insulating region a role in a single dynamical model of electricity and magnetism. In modern language, one may also compare alternative surfaces spanning the same charging circuit: they need not intersect the same conduction current. This last observation is supplied as a **modern consistency test**, not as a claim about Maxwell's own route or notation.

**Task:** Propose one local change to the model that could connect charging in an insulating region to magnetic dynamics. Explain what it preserves, what new assumption it requires, and one alternative interpretation that remains viable. Give a calculation or observation that would test the proposed change. Do not assume that light has already been identified with an electromagnetic wave, that a free electromagnetic wave has been observed, or that a unique material mechanism is known.
